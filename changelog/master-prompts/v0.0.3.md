# Dot Tank Arena Master Prompt — v0.0.3

Status: Expanded Gameplay Foundation  
Next focus: additional maps, dynamic events, destructible props, objective variety, cosmetic progression, and improved bot coordination.

## Project Vision

Build Dot Tank Arena, a multiplayer pixel-art browser game where players control toy tanks in outdoor play environments. The game focuses on constant action, meaningful player interaction, environmental gameplay, progression, and skill-based combat.

The fantasy is kids playing with toy tanks outdoors. Everything should reinforce this identity.

## Core Design Pillars

### Active Arenas

Players should regularly encounter other players, bots, objectives, and environmental features. Arenas should never feel empty.

### Pixel Art First

Tanks, bullets, terrain, obstacles, pickups, UI, and effects must all use pixel art rather than vector-looking placeholders.

### Multiple Paths to Victory

Players can succeed through accurate shooting, positioning, progression, dot collection, dash combat, ring-outs, and objective control.

### Easy to Learn

Movement and controls should feel immediately intuitive. Mastery comes from positioning, timing, dash usage, map awareness, and objective control.

## Technology and Authority

- TypeScript and Vite frontend
- Socket.IO authoritative backend
- Railway backend hosting
- Sites frontend hosting

The server remains authoritative for movement, shooting, damage, XP, dashes, ring-outs, and leaderboard state.

## Arenas and Matchmaking

Maintain five arena instances with configurable capacity. Fill active arenas first by selecting the most-populated arena that is not full. Only open another arena when necessary. Target 8–15 active participants per arena.

## Visual Direction

Players control toy tanks in backyard, sandbox, driveway, and playground environments. Use `image-rendering: pixelated` and pixel-style fonts such as Press Start 2P, Silkscreen, or Pixelify Sans.

Tank sprites contain body, turret, and track components and support future skins. Pixel projectiles create sparks, debris, and dust on impact.

## Environment System

Maps should feel like lived-in outdoor play areas rather than empty battlefields.

Cover and terrain include:

- Toy-brick walls, stone borders, and wood blocks that block bullets and create chokepoints.
- Bushes that conceal tanks through faded nameplates or partial visibility; bots understand concealment.
- Rocks as permanent cover.
- Fallen branches as small navigation obstacles.
- Flower patches as visual decoration.
- Dirt and gravel terrain variation.
- Mud as a future movement penalty.

Increase the arena to approximately 1.5–2 times its previous size while maintaining combat density.

## Minimap

Add a minimal, readable pixel minimap showing player position, boundaries, objectives, and event markers. Future additions may include teammates, XP hotspots, and danger zones.

## Combat

Weapons, damage, eliminations, hit detection, and cooldowns remain server authoritative.

### Dash

Space activates dash on desktop; mobile has a dedicated Dash button. Dash is a quick burst with offensive and defensive utility. Increase its force, responsiveness, impact satisfaction, trail effects, impact sparks, and screen shake.

### Stagger

Successful dash impacts may stagger targets. Staggered players cannot move, turn, fire, or dash. Duration scales with impact force:

- Weak: 0.25 seconds
- Medium: 0.4 seconds
- Strong: 0.6 seconds

This makes bump builds viable and creates ring-out and follow-up opportunities.

### Ring-Outs

Maps contain visible fences, sandbox edges, or garden borders. Health drain begins outside the arena and increases over time. A recent attacker receives elimination credit when displacement leads to death.

## Health and Progression

Every five collected dots grants a small heal to reward exploration and reduce endless attrition.

XP comes from dots, damage, eliminations, and future objectives. Levels grant small capped improvements to health, movement speed, fire rate, and dash strength without excessive scaling.

## Bots

Bots should feel human rather than aimbot-like. Replace random movement and rotation with utility decisions. Bots seek goals, navigate intentionally, pursue enemies, and avoid danger.

Aim includes turn-speed limits, reaction delay, inaccuracy, imperfect tracking, and lower firing frequency. Predictive fire may lead targets but remains reasonably inaccurate.

Personalities:

- Hunter focuses eliminations.
- Collector prioritizes dots and XP.
- Berserker fights aggressively at close range.
- Sniper maintains distance.
- Opportunist targets weak enemies.
- Survivor retreats at low health.

Bots understand bushes, walls, objectives, arena edges, and ring-out risks.

## Sandcastle Event

Every five minutes, spawn a destructible sandcastle with a shared health pool. It appears on the minimap, encourages convergence, and becomes a conflict hotspot. Bots recognize and contest it.

Destroying the sandcastle releases a noticeable but non-dominating power-up lasting 90 seconds. Possible effects:

- Reinforced Plastic: +10% health or damage resistance.
- Fresh Batteries: +10% reload speed.
- Turbo Treads: +8% movement speed.
- Heavy Bumper: +15% dash knockback.
- Shock Absorbers: reduced stagger duration.

## Mobile Support

Provide movement and aim joysticks plus Fire and Dash buttons. Support portrait and landscape layouts.

## Reconnect and Moderation

Maintain a 30-second reconnect grace period and restore the same arena, stats, and progression.

Server-authoritative nickname validation includes profanity filtering, leetspeak normalization, repeated-character normalization, and safe-character rules. Rejected names are never stored, displayed, or broadcast.
